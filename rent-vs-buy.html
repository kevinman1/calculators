How This Calculator Works

Buying: you pay the down payment, transfer tax and other fees up front, then loan or installment payments, property tax and upkeep. When you sell, selling costs and any capital gains tax come off the sale price and the remaining loan is repaid.

Renting: you pay a deposit (returned when you leave) and monthly rent that rises each year.

Fair comparison: whichever option costs less each month, or needs less cash up front, puts the difference into savings earning your chosen return. At the end, both sides are compared as total wealth.

💡 Tip: Compare a rent with the price of a similar home. A gross rental yield well below your loan interest rate usually favors renting; fast price growth or a long stay favors buying.

Defaults are typical estimates, not offers or advice. Rules and fees change, so confirm taxes with the General Department of Taxation and costs with your bank, notary and developer. The calculator ignores utilities, furnishing and the personal value of owning your home.
